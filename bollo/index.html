Connecting golf practice to life beyond XR

Bollo is an XR exercise concept for adults in their late 50s to mid-60s. Over nine months, I worked with one other designer on research and UX systems, and led the 3D UI and spatial interactions.

The Problem
Participants described uncertainty about their changing bodies and a disconnect between exercise platforms and daily life.
the question
How might XR help people adapt exercise to their changing bodies and carry its value into daily life?
our answer
We developed Bollo through a concept video and selected AR and camera-based prototypes, pairing body-specific practice with ways to use exercise data in daily life.

LESSON I LEARNED
Learning what makes an XR coach worth returning to
Value extends beyond the session
I learned to consider what makes an experience worth returning to. For Bollo, this meant extending the value of exercise data into everyday decisions, from choosing suitable products to communicating with healthcare professionals. This shifted my focus from designing a training session to supporting an ongoing relationship with one’s body.
An AI coach needs more than a personality
Designing Marimo taught me to connect character design with interaction logic: when the coach appears, how it speaks, and what data informs its guidance. Considering available tracking technologies alongside these decisions helped me think more concretely about how the coaching experience could work.
Concept feedback is only a starting point
The exhibition helped me understand how people perceived Bollo, but videos and prototypes offered limited insight into actual use. I learned to distinguish feedback on the concept from evidence about usability. My next step would be to test whether users can understand and act on the feedback while swinging.
