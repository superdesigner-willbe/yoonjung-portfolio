OVERVIEW

Our pilot study suggested that participants often looked near the vehicle icon while overlooking status text at the top of the display. We used these observations to design contextual messages closer to the vehicle icon.

We then compared the baseline and redesigned interfaces across eight driving scenarios, analyzing data from 22 participants. Some attention and recognition measures favored the redesign, but the results varied by scenario, and we did not find statistically significant improvements in overall satisfaction or trust.

PROBLEM

Could passengers find the explanation when the vehicle behaved unexpectedly?

Upper-screen text display

In the footage we studied, some explanations appeared as text near the top of the passenger display, while other situations offered limited information about why the vehicle was stopping or changing its route.

We wanted to understand whether participants noticed these messages and how they interpreted the vehicle’s behavior.

SOLUTION

Bringing vehicle-status explanations closer to the vehicle icon

We designed contextual callouts that pair short explanations with animated icons near the vehicle icon. The design aimed to make the reason for a stop or route change easier to locate.

UI Structure

    2. Place explanations near the vehicle icon

EVALUATION & FINDINGS

Comparing the redesign with the baseline

We conducted a screen-based, between-subjects comparison across eight driving scenarios. Of 23 recruited participants, 22 were included in the analysis: 11 viewed the baseline interface and 11 viewed the redesign.

We examined gaze patterns, situation recognition, perceived ease of finding information, satisfaction, and trust, alongside post-study interviews.

FINDING 01

Recognition varied by scenario

The redesigned group had more correct recognition responses in some scenarios and fewer in others. These descriptive results do not establish a consistent or statistically significant improvement.
FINDING 02

Satisfaction and trust showed no significant differences

Overall satisfaction and trust did not differ significantly between the two conditions. Although average trust was lower in the redesigned group, the study did not establish a decrease in trust.

Points show group means; error bars show 95% confidence intervals. Both measures used a 7-point scale, with 11 participants per condition.

LESSON I LEARNED

Distinguishing attention, understanding, and trust

Use observation to guide design, then test the assumption

The pilot showed where participants looked and helped us decide where to place explanations. The follow-up study reminded me that attracting attention does not, by itself, establish better understanding or greater trust.

Combine behavioral and self-reported evidence

Eye tracking showed where participants looked, while interviews and questionnaires helped us examine how they interpreted and felt about the experience. These methods addressed different questions and needed to be interpreted together.

What I would test next

I would vary placement, wording, and motion separately, and assess understanding immediately after each scenario to reduce reliance on later recall.
